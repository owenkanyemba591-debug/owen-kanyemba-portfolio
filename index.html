02 / ABOUT

Curiosity is where
the work begins.

A foundation in learning.
A future in technology.

Owen Kanyemba is a technology enthusiast and ICT student from Zomba, Malawi, with a growing passion for software development, artificial intelligence, computer systems, IoT and robotics.

His journey in education has taken him through several schools and different areas of study, giving him a broad foundation from which to explore technology and innovation. Owen began his primary education at Bwaila Primary School and later continued at Mponda Primary School. He went on to study at Mtendere National Secondary School and later at Zomba Catholic Secondary School.

After secondary school, he joined the Malawi University of Science and Technology, where he spent one year studying Sustainable Energy Engineering. His academic journey later shifted toward information technology, and he is currently pursuing IT at NACIT.

Owen is interested in understanding how technology works—from computer hardware and software to artificial intelligence, mobile applications, web technologies, IoT systems and robotics. His goal is to continue developing practical technical skills and use technology to create innovative solutions to real-world problems.

PROJECT 04 IN DEVELOPMENT

ROAD SAFETY / VEHICLE-TO-VEHICLE SIGNALS

Smart Warning App.

A planned road-safety concept to send warning signals to vehicles approaching from behind when there is danger ahead. The idea includes exploring how to communicate hazards between nearby vehicles and measure the following vehicle's speed to help drivers respond and reduce collision risk. This concept is under development; its detection, communication and speed-measurement capabilities are not yet validated.

Hazard alertsVehicle signalsSpeed awarenessRoad safety
PROJECT 05 IN DEVELOPMENT

WEARABLES / INTERNET OF THINGS / HEALTH

Wearable Health
Monitoring System.

An early-stage wearable IoT health-monitoring concept exploring whether sensor data could help flag possible warning signs associated with selected major diseases. The proposed scope is the top 20 diseases the project aims to study. It is not a medical diagnostic or validated prediction tool, and any health-related results would require clinical validation before use.
